# Impact of the Lahaina Wildfire on Hawaii's Air Quality
**Author:** Malia de Jesus\
**Course:** CS-150 Community Action Computing

### Thesis Statement
The destructive wildfire that struck Lahaina in early August of 2023 significantly affected and impacted Hawaii's air quality, which led to a visible increase in PM2.5 levels. This project investigates the relationship between the fire and air pollution.

### Context
Wildfires release harmful pollutants into the atmosphere, including fine particulate matter (PM2.5) which can cause significant spikes in air pollution levels in Hawaii. These pollutants pose long-term health risks. The Lahaina fire was the deadliest wildfire and most destructive natural disaster recorded in Hawaii since statehood, with the highest U.S. wildfire death toll
since 1918. Given its magnitude, the Lahaina fire's impact on air quality remains a critical environmental concern.

### Data Visualization
The data visualization will focus on Hawaii's PM2.5 levels before, during, and after the Lahaina wildfire in early August. Using data from the EPA, trends to evaluate how the fire affected air pollution will be assessed.

### Data Source
- Data set was provided from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), which focuses on the air quality measurements in Hawaii.
- Key variable: PM2.5 concentrations (μg/m<sup>3</sup>)
- Timeframe: will include data from July to August to assess the changes before, during, and after the Lahaina fire.

### Strategies from "Storytelling With Data"
- **Context:** Using explanatory analysis to communicate the impact of wildfires to Hawaii's air quality.
- **Choosing an Effective Visual:** Using line charts to focus on the trends of PM2.5 levels before, during, and after the wildfire.
- **Decluttering:** Keeping visualization simple to avoid unnecessary distractions.
- **Annotation:** Using annotation to explain nuances in data and highlight important info to pay attention to.
